SOP-01 TEM lamella lift-outScope and overview

Scope and overview

A TEM lamella is a slab of the specimen thin enough for electrons to pass through: below ~100 nm for high-resolution work. The focused ion beam cuts it out of a chosen site, here a bond pad of a microchip over three copper wiring levels, and thins it on a TEM grid. The electron beam watches every step without damaging the site.

Times are instrument time for the FIB-view chip, computed by the simulator (about 1.5 h in all). On a real instrument add navigation, imaging and drift checks. With a xenon plasma FIB, the trenches take minutes at 60 nA; thinning and polishing follow the same steps.

Terms used

Coincidence point: where the electron and ion beams cross, 52° apart. A sample at eucentric height sits on it: tilting does not move the site, and both beams see the same spot.

Regular cross-section (RCS): a staircase trench, deepest at its face. Cleaning cross-section (CCS): line-by-line milling toward the face, which leaves it flat and clean.

Application file: the material the software assumes to turn a depth into a dose (silicon here). Other materials end up deeper or shallower.

Rules

  • Never image the site with ions before it is capped. Every ion frame sputters and implants.
  • Step the current down with the thickness: the beam's tails must never reach through the lamella.
  • Image the thin lamella with electrons only, and at low dose.
  • Stop at the target thickness. You cannot add material back.
Caution. On a real instrument, follow your facility's safety rules for the gas injection system and the manipulator, and the manufacturer's documentation.

SOP-01 TEM lamella lift-outStep 1 of 8
Step 1

Site, eucentric height and coating

  1. Load the Microchip and open Lamella mode (key -). The stage moves to the coincidence point, tilts to 52°, and centres a bond pad: the site.
  2. Find the eucentric height at 0° first. Centre a feature in the SEM image, tilt to ~10°, and bring it back with Stage Z, not with the stage X/Y. Repeat at 52°. (Training task: Dual beam: find the eucentric height.)
  3. Check that the site is coated (a few nm of carbon or Pt). Uncoated oxide charges under both beams: the electrons make it bloom and streak, the ions make it dark and drift.
  4. Image with ions only at the lowest current that shows the pads (10–25 pA) and only briefly. The ion image (Fig. 1) shows the pads' grains by channelling contrast.
  5. Choose the lamella position: the line that the face of the lamella will follow. Here it runs across the pad and the copper lines below it.

Result. The site is at eucentric height and centred in both beams.

Note. At 52° an error of 10 µm in height shifts the ion spot by 7.9 µm from where the SEM shows it: the trenches would land beside the site.

Steps 2 and 3

The protective platinum cap

  1. Insert the Pt gas needle. Draw a 14 × 2 µm rectangle over the lamella line.
  2. Electron-beam Pt first: 0.25 µm at 5 kV, 3.2 nA. It is slow but puts no ions into the surface.
  3. Then ion-beam Pt: 1.4 µm at 100 pA. Keep the current density near 2–6 pA/µm² (Table 1): above it the beam uses up the precursor and the deposit grows slowly; far above, the ions mill instead of deposit.
  4. Retract the needle. The cap shields the top edge from the beam's tails during all the milling to come, and reduces curtaining.

Step 4

Bulk trenches

  1. Select 9 nA (Ga⁺). With xenon, 60 nA removes the same volume in a third of the time.
  2. Draw a regular cross-section 16 × 10 µm on each side of the cap, leaving a 1.5 µm wall. Each staircase is deepest at its face, next to the wall.
  3. Set 7 µm depth. Run both (about 4.6 min each).
  4. Watch the SEM at 52° (Fig. 5): the faces appear, with the passivation, three copper levels, tungsten vias and, deeper, the silicon.
Note. The depth reached is not the depth asked: the dose comes from the silicon application file, and oxide, copper and tungsten mill at different rates. Sputtered material also redeposits in deep, narrow trenches. Check the depth on the face, not in the software.

Steps 5 and 6

Clean the faces, cut it free, lift it out

Cleaning cross-sections

  1. Select 0.8 nA. Draw a cleaning cross-section 15 × 2.4 µm on each face, its face edge at the final wall position, leaving ~1 µm.
  2. Run both. They remove the redeposited material and the steps the bulk trenches left (Fig. 6).

J-cut

  1. Tilt to ~7° so the beam can reach under the lamella.
  2. At 2.5 nA, cut one side and the bottom, leaving the lamella attached at the other side by a small tab (Fig. 7).

Lift-out

  1. Insert the manipulator needle at eucentric height. Bring its tip to touch the free end of the lamella, seen in both beams.
  2. Weld needle and lamella with ion-beam Pt (a 1 × 1 µm pad).
  3. Cut the remaining tab. Lift the lamella out, retract the stage, and drive to the TEM grid post.
  4. Touch the lamella to the post, weld it with Pt on its side, and cut the needle free (Fig. 8).
Caution. Move the needle only in small steps near the specimen and the post. Charging or drift can throw a free lamella off the needle.

Step 7

Thinning

  1. Tilt so the ion beam grazes each face with a ±1.5° over-tilt: one face at 52° + 1.5°, the other at 52° − 1.5°. The over-tilt cancels the beam's natural taper, which otherwise leaves the lamella wedge-shaped (Fig. 13).
  2. Thin both faces alternately, stepping the current down with the thickness (Table 3). In FIB-view: Lamella controls, set the current and target, then Thin both faces.
  3. After each pass, image the face with the SEM at 5 kV. As the lamella gets thinner than the electrons' range, it brightens and becomes uniform (Figs. 9–11). A gradient from top to bottom means a wedge.
  4. Stop around 100–150 nm before polishing.

Step 8

Low-kV polish

  1. Lower the ion voltage to 5 kV. Re-centre and re-focus the ion beam on the post, not on the lamella.
  2. Polish each face at glancing incidence (over-tilt ~4–5°), 50 pA, for a few minutes: it removes most of the 30 kV damage.
  3. Repeat at 2 kV and ~25 pA.
  4. Image once more at 5 kV with electrons and stop. In FIB-view, Send to TKD checks the crystal quality: Kikuchi patterns sharpen as the damage goes.

About half the depth damaged at normal incidence, from FIB-view's binary-collision Monte Carlo (ZBL potential), for silicon. Xenon's heavier ions stop sooner; argon's lighter ones go deeper at the same energy. Plasma ions also leave no gallium.

Result. In this run: 80 nm at the top, 91 nm three micrometres down, 3.6 nm of amorphous silicon per face, ~73 nm of good crystal. Total ~1.5 h of instrument time.

Practise in FIB-view

Lamella mode runs these steps with real patterns on the chip; the thinning and polishing steps are yours to choose. The training task Lamella: thin to 100 nm hides the true thickness: you judge it from the 5 kV SEM view, rate your result, and then see the truth. With your consent the session is recorded, anonymously, for research on how operators judge thickness.
