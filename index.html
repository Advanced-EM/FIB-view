About this exhibit

How to drive it

  • Click a label on the instrument for its physics card: how it works, typical specifications, how it is calibrated, and live values. Drag the 3D view to orbit, scroll to zoom.
  • Modes (keys 1–9, 0, -, =, and \ for the cryo lamella): the electron beam (SE, in-lens, BSE, EBSD, TKD, EDS), the ion beam (ion image, mill, deposit), workflows (slice & view, lamella, cryo lamella) and the Monte Carlo of electrons or ions. Space pauses, C clarity, L labels, R resets the view, N narration.
  • Ion species: gallium from a liquid-metal source (the finest probe) or xenon, argon, nitrogen and oxygen from a plasma source (microamps of current, no gallium). Compare their milling rates, damage depths and chemistry.
  • Mill and deposit: drag on the ion image to draw a pattern (rectangle, regular or cleaning cross-section, line, circle), then press Start. The electron image beside it watches the trench grow at 52°. In Deposit mode, choose Pt, C or W and the electron or ion beam.
  • Lamella: run the eight steps of a TEM lift-out, then thin the lamella yourself at decreasing currents and polish it at low kV. Slice & view: serial-section a battery cathode (or high-pressure-frozen yeast, on the cryo stage) and segment its phases.
  • Cryo lamella: yeast cells frozen in vitreous ice on a grid, on a −190 °C stage: sputter coat, cold GIS platinum, a grazing milling angle and relief cuts, then mill both sides down to ~200 nm for cryo-electron tomography, without warming the ice (it crystallises) or letting contamination grow.
  • Control panel: press V for the on-screen console (Q/W focus, A/S magnification, Z/X beam current, H/L and K/J multifunction, O/P context, arrows for the stage, Tab for stigmator / beam shift / tilt & Z / pattern size, U to start or stop patterning). The TEM-view Console or any game controller works too; the knobs follow the active beam.
  • Guides: printable procedures for TEM lamella lift-out and Python scripting.
  • AI mode (brain button or /): type commands like “switch to xenon”, “mill a cross-section”, “deposit 1 micron of platinum”, “thin to 100 nm”, “what is the sputter yield?”. Hold M to say them. Connect an AI agent (tools/fibview-mcp) to answer questions and run the instrument with you.
  • Training (T): focus the SEM, focus the ion beam without destroying the sample, find the eucentric height, thin a lamella to 100 nm. With your consent the session is recorded (no personal data) and can be downloaded, replayed, or shared with a Hugging Face research dataset.
  • Python (P): script the instrument with the fibview package, in the browser or on a desktop.

What is computed, and how

Images of both beams are ray-cast through the current surface, so a tilted view sees the faces and walls of milled trenches. Electron images use the SE yield curve and secant law, SE2 from backscattered electrons, Reuter/Arnal η(Z, tilt), channelling, ETD shadowing and the probe budget. Ion images use kinetic ion-induced SE emission (∝ electronic stopping), secondary-ion yields enhanced by oxygen, ion channelling, positive charging of insulators, and the ion probe (source, chromatic and spherical terms, with tails).

Milling removes J·Y per step: sputter yields from measured Ga 30 kV volume yields, scaled to other ions and energies with Sigmund's theory and ZBL nuclear stopping, with Yamamura's angular dependence, channelling, gas chemistry (XeF₂, H₂O, oxygen ions on carbon), redeposition and wall taper. Deposition follows reaction- and precursor-limited regimes minus sputtering. The ion Monte Carlo is a binary-collision code (ZBL potential with a tabulated scattering integral, Lindhard–Scharff electronic stopping, full recoil cascades and surface-binding sputtering). EBSD/TKD, EDS (now with implanted Ga, Xe, Ar) and the electron Monte Carlo are as in the SEM exhibit.
